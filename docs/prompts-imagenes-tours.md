## Dirección compartida

```text
Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: vertical 4:5 tour card illustration for the Kusi Q'umir travel website
Input images: Images 1–3 are style references only; do not copy their exact composition.
Style/medium: refined Andean editorial travel illustration matching the references — painterly gouache and screen-print texture, subtly weathered paper grain, simplified realistic forms, sophisticated contemporary tourism-poster finish.
Lighting/mood: luminous natural Andean light; welcoming, trustworthy and contemplative.
Color palette: mineral slate blue, turquoise/teal brand accents, warm ochre, terracotta and cream; restrained natural saturation.
Composition/framing: portrait 4:5; landmark clearly readable; people secondary to the destination; suitable for cropping inside a web card; no borders.
Constraints: original image; culturally respectful; realistic proportions; geographically plausible; no fantasy elements; no text, letters, signage, logo, watermark, frame or border.
Avoid: photographic realism, 3D render, cartoon caricature, neon colors, stereotypical costume spectacle and crowded mass tourism.
```

## Solicitudes específicas

### City Tour Cusco

```text
Show the living historic center of Cusco where precise dark Inca stonework meets warm colonial architecture, with the monumental zigzag megalithic walls of Sacsayhuamán as the unmistakable focal point and the city and Andes beyond. A local Peruvian guide accompanies a small group of contemporary responsible travelers.
```

### Valle Sagrado de los Incas

```text
Show the majestic Urubamba river valley with extensive agricultural terraces and the monumental hillside fortress and living Inca town of Ollantaytambo as the focal destination. A local Quechua guide and a few travelers follow an ancient stone path in soft golden morning light.
```

### Maras y Moray

```text
Make the concentric circular agricultural terraces of Moray the central subject, descending deep into the earth. Place a small group with a local guide on the upper path and subtly echo the geometric white salt pools of Maras on a distant hillside without creating a split-screen collage.
```

### Machu Picchu Full Day

```text
Accurately portray the Inca citadel terraces and stone structures cradled by steep cloud-forest peaks, with Huayna Picchu’s recognizable profile behind and morning mist lifting from the sanctuary. A guide and a small group observe from a permitted overlook.
```

### Laguna Humantay

```text
Show the intense natural turquoise glacial lagoon at the foot of the sharp snow-covered Humantay massif, with rocky puna and ichu grass. A local trekking guide and three contemporary hikers approach along the trail, secondary to the lake and glacier.
```

### Montaña de 7 Colores

```text
Portray Vinicunca’s broad sloping mineral bands in natural red, ochre, muted yellow, sage green, lavender and terracotta, with snowy Ausangate in the distance. A few llamas graze naturally while a guide leads three hikers on the established high-altitude path; never use fluorescent stripes.
```

### Valle Sur del Cusco

```text
Make Tipón’s Inca water engineering the focus: precise stone agricultural terraces crossed by clear ceremonial channels and flowing fountains. Add the warm South Valley landscape, a small distant colonial church silhouette and a local guide explaining the aqueducts to travelers.
```

### Waqrapukara

```text
Portray the remote ceremonial Inca complex integrated into a horn-shaped twin rock formation above the Apurímac canyon, with stone terraces, trapezoidal niches and a central plaza. A guide leads three hikers on the safe approach trail through high-Andean puna.
```

### Puente Q’eswachaka

```text
Show the living Inca rope bridge hand-braided from pale golden ichu fiber over the Apurímac River gorge, with thick woven handrails and traditional anchoring. A community guide and two travelers cross carefully in single file while another community member waits on the near bank; no wood planks or steel cables.
```

### Montaña Palcoyo

```text
Show three broad, gently rounded mineral-striped mountain ridges and Palcoyo’s Bosque de Piedras with tall wind-carved pinnacles. Use a short, softly sloped path, very few visitors and distant Ausangate; keep it clearly different from the single sharp profile associated with Vinicunca.
```
